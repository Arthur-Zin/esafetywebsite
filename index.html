What is E-Safety?

E-safety means protecting yourself from online harm. It involves keeping personal details private, using secure passwords, and reporting bad behaviour like cyberbullying.

Core Online Safety Rules

  • Protect information: Never share your home address, school, or phone number online.
  • Use strong keys: Make passwords long, unique, and hard for others to guess.
  • Block and report: Cut contact with mean users and report cyberbullying to platforms immediately.

Social Media Safety

  • Private profiles: Set all your social media accounts to private so only trusted friends can see your posts.
  • Friend requests: Never accept friend requests or messages from people you do not know in real life.
  • Think before you post: Once you upload a photo or comment, you lose control of who saves, shares, or screenshots it.

Recognising Online Scams

  • Urgent requests: Be suspicious of messages demanding immediate money or action, even if they pretend to be from family or banks.
  • Suspicious links: Do not click on unexpected links in text messages or emails. They can steal your log-in details.
  • Too good to be true: If an online ad offers free items, expensive prizes, or high payouts for no work, it is a scam.

Digital Footprint & Privacy

  • Permanent records: Your digital footprint is the trail of data you leave behind online. It stays searchable for years.
  • Future impact: University admissions teams and employers routinely check public social profiles before hiring.
  • App permissions: Regularly check your mobile app settings to turn off unnecessary location and microphone tracking.

Where to Get Help

  • E-safety Commissioner: File complaints about cyberbullying or image sharing via the eSafety Commissioner website.
  • Lifeline: Crisis support available 24/7 by calling 13 11 14.
  • Kids helpline: Free, confidential phone and online counselling for ages 5 to 25 at 1800 55 1800 or via the kidshelpline website.
